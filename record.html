In short
  • Four days, 21 to 25 October 2026: every test behind the case study, in order, each with the command that reproduces it.
  • On four sealed onboards of a real 911 GT3 R, the gear is right three times in four (73.3 % in replay, 75.2 % by the live report), against 24.2 % for always guessing the commonest gear. The live dash, deciding as it plays, gets 61.5 %. The report's speed is 1 to 4 % off.
  • It hears 86 to 97 % of upshifts and 24 to 43 % of downshifts. A car without a profile reads at 3 to 8 %. Single lap times are no better than guessing the average lap. Nothing has checked the real car's revs against a logger.
  • Truth comes from screens: the game's display, and the teams' data overlays. Test sets were sealed before any method was tried on them. Every headline was re-derived from the raw traces on 3 November (4.32).
  • Each entry opens with its result; the method is folded beneath it. Project terms are defined under Terms.

1. The question generated

Name the car's gear at each moment from an onboard recording's sound alone.

  • Known car: gear ratios known (in ACC, measured from the HUD).
  • Unknown car: only the class known (GT3, GT4, Cup). This is also the real-car case: the Manthey #911 at the N24 has no published ratios. The plan calls it "the flex" (docs/ONBOARD-PLAN.md).

Speed is computed as rpm × the chosen gear's km/h per 1000 rpm. With the gear right it is within 0.5 to 1.4 % of the HUD; with it wrong, about 20 % off, one gear's step. Nearly all speed error is gear error.

2. The data generated

Where the labels come from generated

ACC on a PS5 has no telemetry output; its HUD shows gear, speed, rpm, pedals and the last lap on every frame. tools/hud.py reads it by template matching, one hand-labelled layout per HUD (tools/hud/acc-ps5.json), into hud.csv at 10 rows a second: the ground truth. Sound and HUD share the video file's clock, within the offsets in 4.12.

  • Timing: 0.1 s, one row; a gear change lies between two rows.
  • Misreads: rare. The McLaren 720S Evo's own dash shows through ACC's gear digit, which read in 6 % of frames until a fallback was added (now 99.7 %). The last field needs smoothing.
  • Scope: the HUD is truth for ACC only, and ACC's sound engine is not a real car. Real-car truth: the #911's official N24 speed traps and, from 4.20, the Porsche teams' broadcast overlay (the overlay).

The development set generated

47 HUD-labelled recordings, all ACC, all driven by the owner: PS5 captures in runs/<id>/ (inventory/manifest.csv) and the owner's YouTube streams in runs-streams/<id>/ (inventory-streams/manifest.csv). Each folder holds hud.csv (the labels), trace.csv (the analyser's moments: rpm, confidence, throttle state, gear, speed) and report.json (the analyser's report, with every shift heard).

The bar, set before any method was tried on them: exact gear 75 % and within one 93 % on the five cars; the heard gear matching the speed-trap groups at 90 % of the #911's traps. Six real-car onboards (the sealed onboards) joined on 24 October (4.20). Every score is logged, good or bad.

What the data cannot show generated

  • One driver: every development recording is the owner's. Other drivers' shifting is tested in 4.16 and 4.17 (inventory-scout/).
  • One game: no other sim.
  • The class: GT3, GT4 and Cup only; no prototype, touring car or road car.
  • Uneven cars: the 992 is about a quarter of the hours, so results are also given per car.

Media and rights generated

Recordings stay on the owner's Mac (clips/, streams/, the PS5 volume); the repository holds numbers derived from them: traces, HUD readings, reports, and octave-band energies at 20 ms (research/flex/bands/), from which the sound cannot be played back. Jobs that need the audio run on the Mac through the job queue (tools/listen.py, jobs/), which pushes back derived files only.

3. How it is scored generated

  • Gear exact: the share of moments where the gear equals the HUD's, counting moments at 30 km/h or more with a gear from 1 to 7, pooled across recordings (long ones weigh more); within one gear allows one gear's difference. Exceptions: 4.26's 75.7 % counts all speeds; 4.28's 62.6 % uses the dash scorer's alignment.
  • Replay: npm run gear-replay re-decides the gear from saved traces in seconds, without audio; traces hold about three moments a second, and --upsample 3 interpolates them to the analyser's rate. Real run: the audio through the whole analyser on the Mac, scored by research/flex/m0_score.py (trace.csv against hud.csv). With the method unchanged they agree within about a point (70.9 % replay, 71.5 % real, PS5 clips).
  • Held out: leave one run out (--shifts loo --climb loo) keeps the scored run out of per-car learning; leave one car out (--unknown …, the M1 and M2 scripts) learns nothing from the scored car.
  • Baselines: guessing among the gears, about 17 %; the commonest gear, 26 to 27 % (development set) and 24.2 % (sealed onboards, 4.31); the ratio-free method without learning, 17 to 22 % (M0, 4.9), the unknown-car baseline.

4.1 Synthetic sound (21 and 22 October) generated

Speed continuity raised the gear on five ACC clips' traces from 26–37 % to 46–63 % and replaced shift counting for known cars.

21–22 October

4.2 →Link

Method and detail

Why. The analyser hears the rpm and the shifts (jumps of one gear's step in the rpm). The first gear reading counted shifts, with upshift-ratio checks. A count drifts: one missed shift leaves every later moment a gear off.

Method. For a known gearbox, the rpm implies a speed in each gear, and speed cannot jump. A ViterbiFinds the most likely sequence of hidden states (here, gears) from noisy clues (the revs heard), keeping only the best path into each gear at each moment. Andrew Viterbi, 1967. over the gears picks the sequence that keeps that speed continuous; a missed shift still shows as a jump of one gear's step. The anchor fixes which gear the path sits in: the speed implied by the rpm's climb on full power (power = mass × acceleration × speed, less drag). Without it, continuity drifts to the low gears, where every jump is smaller.

Result. Synthetic laps: 94 % without the team's ratios. On the first ACC batch with measured gearboxes the count read 19 to 41 %: a third of the HUD's gear changes had no heard shift (downshifts under braking), each leaving the count a gear high. Commit 37f0a3d.

Method and detail
  • The rpm sometimes locked at exactly a third of the true value, with high confidence. A four-stroke engine sounds at every half-order, and a third of the rpm has all of those as its own orders.

4.3 The streams: the anchor was wrong (22 and 23 October) generated

A traction cap on the anchor raised the gear from 37 to 56 % on the streams and from 43 to 63 % on the PS5 clips, which played no part in choosing it.

22–23 October

← 4.24.4 →Link

Method and detail

Test. The 22 streams, each car's gearbox known. The rpm came through YouTube 0.5 to 1.0 % off the HUD, as good as the PS5's; the gear was right 27 to 69 % of the time, pulled to the middle gears (true 1st read as 2nd or 3rd, true 5th and 6th as 4th).

Cause. A slow climb at low speed read as a speed far too high (+83 % at 40 to 80 km/h), so the gear came out one high. In ACC the tyres limit acceleration below about 150 km/h, at about 5 m/s² (measured on the HUD).

Changed. The anchor has a traction cap (tractionMs2 on the ACC profiles), chosen on the streams. Commit ba0a2e2.

4.4 The rate bug (23 October) generated

The tracker's cost per shift depended on the frame rate; fixed, the streams replay at three times the trace rate scores 46.6 %, against 28.5 % before.

23 October

← 4.34.5 →Link

Method and detail

Why. Real runs scored below the replay: 38.6 % against 51.7 % on the same streams. The tracker summed its costs per moment against a fixed cost per shift. The analyser runs at about three times the trace's rate, so there a shift cost a third as much relative to the evidence; replaying at three times the rate reproduced the drop.

Fix. The gear is decided on moments 0.3 s apart at any rate, with the climb measured over 0.3 s: streams 46.4 % at five times the rate, PS5 57.0 %. Commit 12864eb. Every later replay runs with --upsample 3, the tracker's real rate.

4.5 Per-car learning (23 October) generated

Fed the HUD's own rpm the tracker scores only 60 %; learning each car's climb and upshift drops raises the PS5 replay from 57.0 to 70.9 %, run left out.

23 October

← 4.44.6 →Link

Method and detail

Test (--hud-rpm). 90 % of the heard rpm is within 3 % of the HUD's, so the weak part is the anchor: continuity alone cannot tell a path from the same path one gear off.

Method. Three things learned per car from HUD footage, one run held out at a time. They replace guessed physics (power, mass, drag), which put the anchor's speed 12 % off at the median moment, and they make the heard shifts, unused before, evidence for the gear.

  • The climb: full-power acceleration at each speed (median, 10 km/h bins); in each gear the rpm's rate of change is held against a(v)/v. Alone it picks the gear at 74 % of full-power moments (run left out).
  • Upshift drops: the rpm ratio heard at each upshift, per gear pair, with its spread. The heard drop is larger than the gearbox's true step, most in the low gears, and tighter in the high gears (log spread 0.036 at 1→2, 0.005 at 5→6), so a heard shift gives the direction and, by its drop, the gear it left.
  • Gaps: across stretches with no reliable rpm (heavy braking), a drop of several gears costs no more than one gear per half second.

4.7 Ideas tested and not kept generated

Deceleration, sector times and gear whine were tested as gear evidence; none was kept.

Date not given

← 4.64.8 →Link

Method and detail
  • Deceleration, learned like the climb, picks the gear at 34 % of off-power moments.
  • Sector times: race timing lines up with the recording to 0.1 % of a sector's length, but a sector with wrong gears is no further off than the rest; each spans several gears and the errors cancel. Timing can calibrate the speed's scale; it cannot find a wrong gear.
  • Gear whine: a straight-cut gearbox whines at a mesh order that differs per gear. tools/whine.py found no line tied to road speed per HUD gear in three streams; gear-specific lines appear only in the top gears (the 992 in 6th: order 18.2, 20 dB above the floor). In ACC's streamed sound whine is at most a tie-breaker. A real straight-cut box is louder, and the N24 broadcast is the place to look. Open.

4.10 M1: class priors (23 October) generated

Method and detail

Idea. Race gearboxes leave each gear at a similar speed whatever the car: out of 1st the GT3s here shift at 101 to 109 km/h (the Mustang at 122). Cars differ in rev limit, which the sound gives as the upshift rpm. So km/h per 1000 rpm in gear g is the class's speed at the upshift out of g (median over the class's other cars' HUDs) divided by the recording's heard upshift rpm.

Gearbox (python3 research/flex/m1_gearbox.py --out research/flex/k-estimates.json, leave one car out): within 10 % for 90 % of gears; 233 of 247 gears named right. Misses: the two GT4s (one other GT4 to learn from, and they differ), the Mustang (12 % taller than the class), the Cup (7 to 12 % shorter).

Method and detail
