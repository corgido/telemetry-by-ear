Telemetry by ear · the record

The gear from onboard sound: the research record

Read this first. This record was written by the agent as the work happened, as its first attempt at documenting a fast loop: provenance first, purpose second. If this were more than a demo, it would be done the other way round: start from the questions the instrument must answer, and keep each record for the decision it supports.

The speed of agentic coding is the reason it looks like this. Work that once took weeks happened in hours, faster than a person can read it. So the record is collected and kept automatically, and every part says what it is:

  • generated written by the agent, not yet read and confirmed by a person. Most of this page.
  • Derived numbers: computed by a committed script from committed data; the command is given beside them.
  • verified read and confirmed by a person, who is named with the date.
  • Supplied facts: what the human brought from the driver’s seat (below).

An instrument a team relied on would need this as its audit trail, with every label earned rather than defaulted.

Supplied by the human

  • The simulated test laps were too fast for their circuits (seen on the page, 22 October 2026)
  • A tyre's operating window is 80 to 90 °C; 26.8 psi is the usual theoretical optimum (racing knowledge, 23 October 2026)

  • Old versions of the game have a different tyre and physics model: recordings older than two years are left out (ACC-specific racing knowledge, 24 October 2026)

This records how Onboard's gear reading was built and tested: what data it used, how it was scored, each step taken, why each was taken, what each gave, and what failed. It is written so someone else can rerun every number. The work started on 21 October 2026. This version is from 23 October 2026, and new steps are added at the end of section 4.

Nothing published describes reading a car's gear from its onboard sound (see docs/COMPETITORS.md). So there is no prior method to compare against. The comparisons here are against chance, against simple rules, and against earlier versions of the same method.

1. The question generated

Given only the sound of an onboard recording, which gear is the car in at each moment?

There are two versions of the question:

  • A known car: its gear ratios are known (in ACC, measured from the HUD).
  • An unknown car: nothing about it is known except its class (GT3, GT4, Cup). This is "the flex". It is also the real-car case: the Manthey #911 at the N24 has no published ratios.

Speed follows from the gear. With the gear right, the speed from the sound is within 0.5 to 1.4 % of the HUD. With the gear wrong, it is about 20 % off, which is one gear's step. So almost all of the speed error is gear error.

2. The data generated

Where the labels come from generated

ACC on a PS5 has no telemetry output. Its HUD shows the gear, speed, rpm, pedals and the last lap time on every frame. tools/hud.py reads it by template matching, using one hand-labelled layout per HUD (tools/hud/acc-ps5.json). It writes hud.csv at 10 rows a second. That file is the ground truth. It is aligned with the sound because both come from the same video file.

Known limits of the labels:

  • Timing is good to 0.1 s (one HUD row). A gear change is known to lie between two rows.
  • Misreads are rare but exist. The McLaren 720S Evo's own dash shows through behind ACC's gear digit, so it read in only 6 % of frames until a fallback was added (now 99.7 %). The last field misreads often enough to need smoothing.
  • The HUD is truth only for ACC. What ACC's sound engine does is not what a real car does. Real-car truth exists only for the #911 at the N24, via the official speed traps.

The development set generated

There are 47 HUD-labelled recordings, all of ACC and all driven by the owner. The analyser's output for each is in runs/<id>/ (PS5 captures, manifest inventory/manifest.csv) and runs-streams/<id>/ (the owner's YouTube streams, manifest inventory-streams/manifest.csv). Each folder holds:

  • hud.csv: the labels;
  • trace.csv: the analyser's moments: rpm, confidence, throttle state, gear, speed;
  • report.json: the analyser's report, including every shift it heard.

The sealed test set generated

These are kept apart so the final claim is honest:

  • The drive/data collection playlist's five cars at the Red Bull Ring: AMG GT3, R8 Evo II, 296 GT3, GT-R GT3, 720S GT3 Evo. They are listed in inventory-drive/manifest.csv. Only their HUDs have been read (runs-drive/). Their sound has not been heard. No profile, tuning or learning uses them.
  • The #911 at the N24 (2026), from a broadcast-compiled/captured onboard. Its truth is the official speed traps, laid on the recording by tools/n24.py.

The bar was set before any method was tried on them:

  • exact gear 75 % and within one gear 93 % on the five cars;
  • the heard gear agreeing with the speed-trap clusters at 90 % of traps on the #911.

The test is scored once per method version, and every score is logged, good or bad.

What the data cannot show generated

  • One driver. Every development recording is the owner's driving, so a driver who shifts differently (earlier, later, short-shifting, holding gears) is untested. Recordings from other people (inventory-scout/, being gathered) are the remedy.
  • One game. No other sim is in the data.
  • The class. All of it is GT3, GT4 and the Cup. There is no prototype, touring car or road car.
  • Uneven cars. The 992 alone is about a quarter of the hours. Pooled numbers lean towards it, so results are also given per car.

Media and rights generated

Recordings never enter the repository; they stay on the owner's Mac (clips/, streams/, the PS5 volume). The repository holds derived numbers only: traces, HUD readings, reports, and octave-band energies at 20 ms (research/flex/bands/), from which the sound can't be played back. The Mac runs jobs that need the audio through the job queue (tools/listen.py, jobs/), and pushes back only those derived files.

3. How it is scored generated

  • Gear exact: the share of moments where the gear given equals the HUD's gear. A moment counts when the HUD shows 30 km/h or more and a gear from 1 to 7. Moments are pooled across recordings, so long recordings weigh more.
  • Within one gear: the same, allowing a difference of one.
  • Replay vs real run.
    • npm run gear-replay re-decides the gear from the saved traces in seconds, with no audio. Its traces hold about three moments a second, and --upsample 3 interpolates them to the analyser's real rate.
    • A real run puts the audio through the whole analyser on the Mac. It is scored by research/flex/m0_score.py (trace.csv against hud.csv).
    • The two agree to within about one point when the method is unchanged (for example 70.9 % replay against 71.5 % real on the PS5 clips).
  • Held out.
    • Leave one run out (--shifts loo --climb loo): what is learned per car is learned without the run being scored.
    • Leave one car out (--unknown …, and M1/M2's scripts): nothing is learned from the car being scored.
  • Baselines.
    • Guessing among the gears scores about 17 %.
    • Always answering the commonest gear scores 26 to 27 %.
    • Without ratios and without learning, the method itself scores 17 to 22 % (M0, section 4.11). That is the baseline for an unknown car.

4. The record generated

Each step gives four things: the problem seen, what was reached for and why, what it gave, and what it changed or ruled out. Numbers are the ones measured at the time. Where a number from that time no longer reproduces from today's code, this is said.

4.1 On synthetic sound: counting shifts, then speed continuity (21 and 22 October) generated

Problem. The analyser hears the engine's rpm and the shifts (jumps of a gear's step in the rpm). The first gear reading counted those shifts.

Reached for. First, counting with upshift-ratio checks. Then, for a car with a known gearbox, the speed's continuity.

  • In each gear the rpm implies a speed, and the speed can't jump.
  • A Viterbi [@claude: this needs a hover/tap definition] over the gears picks the sequence that keeps the speed continuous across the moments the rpm is known.
  • A missed shift still shows, as a jump of one gear's step.
  • It is anchored by the speed the rpm's climb implies on full power (power = mass × acceleration × speed, less drag), because continuity alone drifts to the low gears, where every jump is smaller.

Why. A count drifts: one missed shift leaves every later moment one gear off until something resets it.

Gave.

  • On synthetic laps: 94 % without the team's ratios.
  • On the first ACC batch with measured gearboxes, the count read the gear right only 19 to 41 % of the time. A third of the HUD's gear changes had no heard shift (downshifts under braking), and each left the count a gear high.
  • Speed continuity on five clips' traces gave 46 to 63 % (from 26 to 37 %). Commit 37f0a3d.

Changed. Gear reading moved from counting to continuity, for known cars.

4.2 The first real sound (22 October) generated

Problem. A 13-minute tab recording of the #911's N24 start was the first real engine.

Found.

  • The rpm sometimes locked at exactly a third of the true value, with high confidence. A four-stroke engine sounds at every half-order, and a third of the rpm has all of them as its own orders.
  • Downshifts a third of a second apart were merged into one jump, which then failed the ratio check.

Changed. Both were fixed in the analyser (docs/ONBOARD.md, "Every half-order"). This is where it was first seen that downshifts under braking are the weak point. Section 4.13 measures it.

4.3 The streams: the error is all in the gear, and the anchor was wrong (22 and 23 October) generated

Problem. The 22 streams were heard with each car's gearbox known.

  • The rpm came through YouTube 0.5 to 1.0 % off the HUD, as good as the PS5's.
  • The gear was right only 27 to 69 % of the time.
  • The errors were pulled towards the middle gears: true 1st read as 2nd or 3rd, and true 5th and 6th read as 4th.

Reached for. The anchor. A slow climb at low speed read as a speed far too high (+83 % at 40 to 80 km/h), so the gear came out one too high. In ACC the tyres, not the engine, limit acceleration below about 150 km/h, at about 5 m/s² (measured on the HUD). So the anchor got a traction cap (tractionMs2 on the ACC profiles).

Gave.

  • Streams: 37 % to 56 % (the cap was chosen on the streams).
  • PS5 clips: 43 % to 63 %. The PS5 clips played no part in choosing it.
  • Commit ba0a2e2.

4.4 The rate bug (23 October) generated

Problem. The full run on the audio scored below the replay: 38.6 % against 51.7 % on the same streams. The tracker summed its costs per moment against a fixed cost per shift. The analyser runs at about three times the trace's rate, so at the real rate the cost of a shift was three times cheaper relative to the evidence. Replaying at three times the rate reproduced the drop: 28.5 %.

Reached for. Deciding the gear on moments 0.3 s apart, whatever the rate, with the climb measured over 0.3 s.

Gave. Streams 46.6 % at three times the rate and 46.4 % at five; PS5 57.0 %. Commit 12864eb.

Changed. From here on, every replay is run with --upsample 3. The replay scores the tracker, so it must run at the tracker's real rate.

4.5 Is it the rpm or the tracker? (23 October) generated

Problem. Was the weak part hearing the rpm, or turning rpm into gear?

Test. Feed the HUD's own rpm into the tracker (--hud-rpm). That gave 60 %. Meanwhile 90 % of the heard rpm is within 3 % of the HUD's. So the tracker was the limit, in particular its absolute anchor: continuity alone can't tell a path from the same path one gear off.

Reached for. Learning three things per car from the HUD footage, held out one run at a time:

  • The climb: the car's acceleration on full power at each speed (median, 10 km/h bins). In each gear the rpm's rate of change is held against a(v)/v. On its own this picks the gear at 74 % of full-power moments (leave one run out).
  • The upshift drops: the ratio heard at each upshift, per gear pair, with its spread. The heard drop is larger than the gearbox's true step, most of all in the low gears, and tighter in the high gears (log spread 0.036 at 1→2 and 0.005 at 5→6). So a heard shift says which way the gear went and, by its drop, from which gear.
  • Gaps: across stretches with no reliable rpm (heavy braking), a drop of several gears costs nothing extra beyond one gear per half second.

Why these. They replace guessed physics (power, mass, drag), which put the anchor's speed 12 % off at the median moment, with measured behaviour. They also turn the heard shifts into evidence for the gear. Before, the heard shifts were not used at all.

Gave (replay at the real rate):

physics onlylearned, run left outlearned from all
streams (22)46.6 %58.0 %66.0 %
PS5 clips (25)57.0 %70.9 %70.9 %

The tracker's weights (SHIFT_EVIDENCE in src/analysis/track.ts) were chosen by sweeps on the streams with runs left out, for example climbSigma 0.25 and gapPerShift 0.5. The PS5 clips served as the check. Commit ca28e80.

Real runs with the learned profiles (the listener's jobs ps5-redo-2 and streams-redo-3):

  • PS5 71.5 % exact, 90.8 % within one;
  • streams 67.2 % exact, 88.6 % within one.

4.6 Where the errors sit (23 October) generated

Test. The PS5 HUD's pedal fields were read (throttle, brake, ABS by the brake bar's colour), and the gear was scored by what the pedals were doing.

What it showed. The gear is good on power and poor under braking. Every braking zone has two good anchors around it: the gear at the end of the straight, and the gear once full throttle returns. Counting the downshifts between those two is bounded at both ends, so a missed downshift can't carry on. This is the owner's "make it count" idea, used where counting is safe. It became M3.

4.7 Ideas tested and not kept generated

  • Deceleration names the gear? Learned like the climb, it picks the gear at only 34 % of moments off power. Not kept.
  • Sector times check the gear? Race timing lines up with the recording to 0.1 % of a sector's length. But a sector with wrong gears in it is not further off than the rest: each sector spans several gears and the errors cancel. Timing can calibrate the speed's overall scale, not find a wrong gear. Not kept.
  • Gear whine? A straight-cut gearbox whines at a mesh order that differs per gear, which would name the gear directly. tools/whine.py looked for such lines per HUD gear in three streams and found no line tied to road speed. Gear-specific lines show only in the top gears (the 992 in 6th has one at order 18.2, 20 dB above the floor). So in ACC's sound, as streamed, whine is at most a tie-breaker. A real straight-cut box is louder, and the N24 broadcast is the place to look. Open.

4.8 The flex is planned and the benchmark sealed (23 October) generated

The question became the unknown car, and the benchmark (section 2) was sealed before any unknown-car method was tried. The plan is in docs/ONBOARD-PLAN.md ("The flex"): class priors (M1), a learned shift detector (M2), counting between anchors and laps voting (M3), then the sealed test once (M4).

The rule that guided it was "the class, never the car": every prior is pooled over the other cars of the class.

4.9 M0: what an unknown car scores today (23 October) generated

Question. Does the ratio-free path score better than the ratio tracker? That path counts the gear, then lets physics decide, with no gearbox. It was the earlier "Manthey setting", and on one race (the TiTan Cup at wet Laguna Seca) it had looked better.

Test. All 47 clips were heard again without the profiles' ratios (the listener's jobs blind-ps5-1 and blind-streams-1, in runs-blind/ and runs-streams-blind/), and scored with python3 research/flex/m0_score.py.

exactwithin onespeed error (median)
PS5, ratio tracker72.4 %90.8 %0.7 %
PS5, ratio-free21.8 %63.6 %13.5 %
streams, ratio tracker68.2 %88.6 %1.0 %
streams, ratio-free17.1 %51.8 %16.4 %

What it showed.

  • Every car does worse without ratios. Only top gear holds up (49 to 61 %).
  • The ratio-free path's two parts both fail: the count drifts at missed shifts, and the physics' power and mass guesses are too rough to decide the gear.
  • A count pinned to ±1 per heard shift scored 15 to 19 %. That was measured inline and not kept as a script.
  • The TiTan race wasn't a sign of anything about the method. It is stream 7, and its HUD gearing matches the PS5 Cup clips within about 1 % in every gear. So the profile was the right car (the first guess had been a wrong Cup generation). The likelier reason it looked better there is a dry climb curve meeting a wet track.

Changed.

  • About 20 % exact is the baseline for an unknown car.
  • The ratio tracker can't run on an unknown car at all, because it needs the gearbox.
  • The plan replaces both parts of the ratio-free path: the count, with a learned detector (M2) and anchored counting (M3); the physics guesses, with class priors (M1).

4.10 M1: an unknown car's gearbox from its class and its own sound (23 October) generated

Idea. Race gearboxes are laid out so that each gear is left at a similar speed, whatever the car. Out of 1st, the GT3s here shift at 101 to 109 km/h (the Mustang at 122). What differs between cars is the rev limit, and the sound gives that, as the rpm the driver upshifts at. So an unknown car's km/h per 1000 rpm in gear g is the class's speed at the upshift out of g (the median over the other cars of the class, from their HUDs) divided by the recording's heard upshift rpm.

Why. It needs nothing from the car except its sound, and one number per class per gear.

Gearbox. python3 research/flex/m1_gearbox.py --out research/flex/k-estimates.json, leaving one car out:

  • 1.8 % off at the median, and within 10 % for 90 % of gears;
  • 233 of 247 gears named right.
  • Misses: the two GT4s (only one other GT4 to learn from, and they differ), the Mustang (12 % taller than the class) and the Cup (7 to 12 % shorter).

Gear (replay as an unknown car: the estimated gearbox, the climb and upshift drops pooled over the other cars of the class, and the gear count and rev limit from the estimate):

ratio-free (M0)unknown-car modethe car's own profile
PS5 clips21.8 %68.4 %72.4 %
streams17.1 %52.5 %68.2 %

By car, held out: 992 GT3 R 70.8 %, Huracán 70.2 %, M4 65.1 %, Bentley 58.6 %, Cup 47.8 %, AMG GT4 39.7 %, Mustang 37.1 %, Aston GT4 19.5 %. The GT3s and the Cup together score 63.3 %.

Tried and failed: refitting the scale from the climb (--refit).

  • Why it was tried: the worst cars are those whose gearing estimate is off by a uniform scale.
  • Result: 41.4 % (streams) and 47.2 % (PS5), worse than not refitting, and the fitted scales ran to the edges of the range searched.
  • Reason: a uniform change of scale and a relabelling of the gears cancel out. Scaled up 15 %, the same stretch reads one gear lower, and the climb agrees just as well. The scale can't be identified from the climb. An unusually geared car needs another clue, such as the speed at its shifts or the downshift pattern (M2).
  • The option stays in the code, off.

A confound, still open. These traces were heard by the analyser with each car's own profile (cylinder count, rev range, shift band). A truly unknown car would be heard with a class profile. Before the sealed test, the development set is to be heard again that way, and this table redone.

4.11 M2, first step: what a shift sounds like (23 October) generated

Question. The plan listed possible "affirmers" of a shift beyond the pitch jump: the ignition cut on upshifts, the auto-blip on downshifts, the gearbox's clunk. Which of them are actually in the sound?

Reached for. Averaging, the cheapest test there is. tools/shift_probe.py takes every HUD gear change at speed in a recording. It times each by the analyser's heard shift when one lies within 0.3 s, and otherwise by the HUD. It then averages three envelopes at 2 ms resolution from -0.6 to +0.6 s around each change, in dB relative to the level before:

  • the whole level;
  • a high band, 2 to 6 kHz;
  • a low band, 60 to 400 Hz.

Groups: upshifts heard and missed, downshifts heard and missed, and the same number of random moments at speed. It was run on 3 PS5 clips and 4 streams (jobs probe-*). Results are in research/flex/probe/<run>/probe.json.

runupshifts hearddownshifts heardmissed downshifts: high band's rise (dB, ms after HUD)random moments
PS5 2 (M4)92 %24 %+1.6 at 196+0.1
PS5 7 (992)97 %31 %+2.5 at 74+0.2
PS5 22 (Cup)86 %43 %+1.8 at 144+0.1
stream 1 (Bentley)65 %18 %+2.0 at 178+0.1
stream 2 (Huracán)97 %35 %+2.9 at 164+0.2
stream 3 (Mustang)83 %22 %+2.1 at 176+0.2

The rise was measured on 20 ms smoothed medians, as the highest point from 0 to +350 ms. The random column is the same measure on the random moments.

What it showed.

  • The gap is the downshifts. Most upshifts are heard, but only 18 to 43 % of downshifts. The analyser listens for a jump in pitch, and under braking the rpm is already moving.
  • Missed downshifts leave a mark anyway. In all seven runs, the high band rises 1.6 to 2.9 dB, 75 to 240 ms after the HUD changes gear. That is the blip and the rpm climbing into the lower gear.
  • Upshifts: the level dips 1.4 to 3.7 dB around the heard shift.
  • No sharp cut or clunk shows. The HUD's 0.1 s timing would average away a click of a few ms. Chasing it needs alignment on each event, which isn't worth doing before the downshift mark is used.

Changed. M2's target became the downshifts. The upshifts are already heard.

4.12 M2, second step: a learned downshift detector (23 October) generated

Reached for. A classifier on the shape of the sound around each moment, trained on the HUD's gear changes and tested leave one car out.

Design and why:

  • Material. Each development recording's energy in eight octave bands (60 Hz to 12 kHz), every 20 ms, as uint8 dB (tools/shift_probe.py --bands-all; 23 MB for all 47 recordings in research/flex/bands/).
    • Octave bands at 20 ms keep the blip's spectral shape and timing, and can't be played back as the sound.
    • Keeping the bands in the repository lets the detector be retrained here in minutes rather than via a Mac job each time. The owner agreed to the size.
  • Steps and labels. Every 0.1 s step of the HUD at over 40 km/h is labelled up, down or nothing by the HUD's gear change in it.
    • Steps beside a change (within two rows) are not used as negatives, since the HUD's timing is good to one row.
    • Gear changes of more than one are dropped.
  • Features. The eight bands from -0.6 to +0.6 s around the step, averaged into 40 ms bins: 240 numbers. Each band is relative to its own level in the first 0.2 s, so the mix and the recording's gain drop out. That matters because a wild user's mix is unknown (section 2).
  • Model. Multinomial logistic regression in numpy, with balanced class weights, L2 regularisation and Adam.
    • It is the simplest model that can weigh a shape.
    • It is 723 weights, so it ports to the browser page as a dot product.
    • A bigger model is only worth it if this one falls short.
  • Training data. All positives, and 8 % of the nothing-steps.
  • Testing. Leave one car out: trained on the other seven cars, run over every step of the held-out car.

The first scoring was wrong.

  • The first run reported upshifts found 1 to 3 % of the time and downshifts 2 to 38 %.
  • Checking one held-out car showed the model was fine. On the Huracán, a downshift step scores above a non-shift step 98 to 99 % of the time (AUC 0.98 to 0.99).
  • The scoring was at fault. The cut for "one false alarm a minute" was taken on probabilities, which saturate near 1 and tie, so the cut landed above most true events.
  • The scoring now uses the log of the odds against "nothing". It reports AUC, plus the share of events found at 0.5, 1 and 2 false alarms a minute.

Gave, first run (labels on the HUD's clock; research/flex/m2-detector.json):

downshifts11,49232.6 %0.82515.2 %42.1 % / 45.7 %

By car the downshift AUC ran from 0.98 (Huracán, Mustang) and 0.97 (Cup) down to 0.75 (M4) and 0.53 (AMG GT4, chance).

Why the AMG GT4 failed: the labels' clock.

  • The question. Was the AMG GT4 at chance because its sound differs, or because of its labels?
  • The test. For each run, the median gap between each HUD upshift and the analyser's nearest heard upshift.
    • Most runs: the heard shift comes 70 to 220 ms after the HUD's. PS5 clips 70 to 150 ms; streams 110 to 220 ms.
    • Stream 18, the only AMG GT4 recording: 190 ms before.
  • What it means. The sound and the HUD's digit are not offset the same way in every recording (the capture and the YouTube encode both play a part). A model that looks at a fixed window around the HUD's step sees the AMG's blip about 300 ms away from where it learned to expect it.
  • The fix (--align):
    • each run's steps are moved onto its own sound's clock, by its own median offset less 120 ms (the typical one);
    • the offset comes from the run's heard upshifts and its HUD only, so it corrects the labels, not the detector;
    • five of the Huracán's 20-second PS5 clips have too few upshifts to measure it and stay as they were.

4.18 The real #911's gear at its N24 speed traps: 37 % right, the top gears squashed (24 October 2026) generated

Why. Everything the gear has been scored on was ACC. The #911's N24 run (runs-n24/full) already holds a real-car truth for the gear: at each speed trap the official km/h over the rpm heard falls into one of four groups (record of 23 October, tools/n24.py), which are gears 3 to 6.

What. python3 research/flex/n24_trap_gear.py: 164 traps; the run's gear is right at 61 (37 %), within one at 145 (88 %). Sixth is right (29 of 33); fifth is read as sixth 59 times of 80; fourth as sixth 17 times of 48. The run's gearbox came from physics (the speed the revs' climb implies): its km/h per 1000 rpm, 14.1, 17.8, 22.2, 24.0, 23.7, 26.4, has fifth below fourth and sixth 16 % short of the real 31.4, so the top three gears blur together and the tracker settles on the top.

What it means. On the real car the gear without ratios is the weak reading, as in ACC, and the physics gearbox is the cause here. The straights' fall to third that the owner saw is the live page's version of the same weakness: the gear there is the shifts counted plus an offset re-decided every few seconds from that gearbox, and on a long straight near top speed the climb is too small to say much (to be checked on the owner's export).

The Döttinger Höhe. The owner's export of that live run (499 s) showed a second fault: flat out at the end of the Döttinger Höhe the revs sat at 8,630 to 8,750 rpm and the confidence fell from 0.98 to nothing, so the dash went blank. The held-note cue (a held note is not an engine) let an engine keep its motion only while its revs crept up; at top speed they stop. Now an engine that earned its motion and sits flat in the top fifth of its range keeps it for up to 75 s after it last travelled (src/analysis/engine.ts). A test of 25 s flat at 8,700 rpm was sure 5.8 % of the time before the change and passes after; the music tests still read as no engine.

The fix, without spoiling the test. The real 992 R's km/h per 1000 rpm in each gear, put into its profile, makes the gear absolute. Taking it from these traps would make the N24 its own test, so it is taken from the real-car round's tuning clips instead (inventory-real/: the overlay's gear and speed, the rpm heard), and the N24 traps stay the sealed check: this 37 % is the before.

4.19 Heard as the wrong car, the revs read at a fixed multiple; the page now says so (24 October 2026) generated

Why. The owner tried the synthetic Monza lap with the V8 chosen and it read badly; with the V10, close.

What. Synthetic Monza (one lap, seed 7) heard as each car: as its own car (the test flat-six) revs 98.4 % within 2 %, gear 97.6 %; as the V8, 66.0 % and 8.8 %.
